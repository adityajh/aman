# Market Analysis — Solo Therapist Software
## India and global, with a focus on measurement-based care

*July 2026. All third-party pricing sourced from public vendor pages and published comparisons; verify before quoting externally.*

---

## 1. Headline findings

1. **Outcome *tracking* is commoditised globally.** Deliver a measure → auto-score → graph is now table stakes in the global mid-tier. Greenspace, Blueprint, TherapyNotes, TheraPlatform and SimplePractice all do it.
2. **Outcome *trajectory alerting* is not.** Comparing a client against an expected recovery curve and flagging predicted treatment failure has exactly one small-practice option worldwide: OQ-Analyst. Aman's Predicted Progress is the same mechanism.
3. **Cohort-derived prediction that improves with platform scale is unclaimed** by anyone at solo price points.
4. **None of this exists in India.** No Indian platform surfaced doing measurement-based care in any form. The category is absent, not merely underserved.
5. **Aman Pro is materially underpriced.** The global MBC band for solo practitioners is $25–40/month (₹2,100–3,400) for measurement *alone*.
6. **Aman has one serious product gap:** every credible MBC competitor delivers the measure to the client automatically. Aman requires the counsellor to enter scores by hand.
7. **The admin layer is worth ₹0.** Zoho gives away scheduling and invoicing permanently, in India, GST-native. See §3.5. This is the finding that most changes the plan.

**Caveat on the right-hand column:** "not observed" means their public marketing does not mention it. That is a strong signal but not verification. Confirm before making any public claim.

### What this means

The Indian market has solved *admin* for therapists and has not touched *effectiveness*. PractiPal's own positioning is explicitly about scheduling, payments and reducing non-clinical time. Nobody is selling a therapist the ability to answer "are my clients getting better?"

Whether that's an opportunity or an absence of demand is the central unresolved question — see §6.

## 3.5 Zoho — the free-forever floor

The most serious competitor to the Practice tier is not a therapy product at all.

### The free stack

| Product | Cost | What a solo therapist gets |
|---|---|---|
| **Zoho Invoice** | **Free forever** | Invoices, credit notes, automated payment reminders, online and offline payment collection, **expense recording**, **client self-service portal**, work-hour logging, multiple reports |
| **Zoho Bookings** | **Free plan, one user** | Unlimited customers, unlimited appointments, booking page, two-way calendar sync (Zoho/Google/Microsoft), automated confirmations and reminders, customisable booking form, mobile app |
| **Zoho Forms** | Free tier | Intake forms |

**Total: ₹0/month.**

### Zoho One, if they want everything

₹1,250–1,500 per employee per month (All-Employee, annual, +18% GST) or ₹3,500 per user (Flexible). A solo practitioner is one employee, so All-Employee applies — roughly ₹1,475–1,770/month all-in for 45+ apps including CRM, Books and Analytics.

### Why this is worse for Aman than any therapy competitor

- **Indian company, Chennai-headquartered.** No foreign-vendor or data-residency objection
- **GST-native and ITC-eligible** — a real, recurring pain Aman does not address at all
- **Enormous trust and brand recognition** among Indian small businesses and professionals
- **It's free**, not cheap. There is no price at which Aman Practice wins this comparison

### What Zoho cannot do

Everything clinical. Zoho Invoice bills a *customer*; it does not hold a *clinical record*. There is no structured session note, no clinical history, no termination workflow, no per-session fee logic (pro-rata, cancellation bands), no outcome measurement, no deterioration detection, no trajectory prediction. Zoho is generic business software with no clinical layer whatsoever, and no plausible path to one.

### The conclusion

**The administrative layer of a solo therapy practice is a commodity approaching zero, and Aman should stop trying to sell it.**

This reframes the whole product. Billing, invoicing and scheduling are not the value — they are the *convenience of not stitching four generic tools together*, which is worth something but not ₹999/month against free.

Two viable responses:

**A. Kill the Practice tier.** Single product, Pro at ₹1,999. Coherent, honest, and removes the feature-gating, upgrade flows and support overhead that a low-time operator can least afford. **Recommended.**

**B. Keep Practice, but sell it on clinical documentation, not billing.** Structured session notes, clinical history, termination workflow, per-session fee logic — none of which Zoho has. The line becomes: *Zoho can send your invoices. It can't hold your clinical record.* Defensible, but it still asks ₹999 for something adjacent to free.

Option C — making Practice free — is rejected: it contradicts the no-free-tier discipline and imports exactly the support load the operating model is designed to avoid.

## 4. Global — measurement-based care specialists

These are Aman's real competitors on the differentiator, not PractiPal.

| Player | Solo price | What it does | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OQ-Analyst** | **From $250/yr** (~₹21,000) | OQ-45 family. Empirically derived alerts comparing client trajectory against expected recovery curves; flags clients at risk of treatment failure. The mechanism from Lambert's research programme shown to reduce deterioration | Described as feeling like the research tool it is. No client engagement layer. Instruments licensed individually. Runs alongside an EHR, not instead of one |
| **Greenspace** | **$24.99/mo** Basic, $39.99 Premium (~₹2,100 / ₹3,400) | Dedicated MBC. Automated delivery, instant scoring, progress graphs shared with the client | Measurement layer only — notes, billing and therapy delivery live elsewhere. Fixed assessment library; no custom scales |
| **Blueprint** | **Free Core EHR**; AI $0.99–1.49/session | Large auto-scored library (PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, hundreds more), automated delivery, AI documentation. Reports 70,000+ professionals | Centre of gravity moved from measurement to AI documentation. US/Canada compliance framing |
| **Quenza** | **$25/mo** (~₹2,100) | Build any scale you're licensed to use; schedule inside a client-facing Pathway; mobile app | Explicitly not a psychometric analytics engine — no norm-referenced dashboards, no automated cutoff alerts, no benchmarking |
| **Mirah** | Sales-quoted | 500+ measures, org-scale analytics, CoCM billing | Not built or priced for individuals |
| **Owl (NeuroFlow)** | Sales-quoted | Enterprise, population analytics, payer programmes | Health systems only |

### The key read

**OQ-Analyst is the closest thing to Aman's Predicted Progress that exists.** It is the research gold standard, it is 30 years old, and it is the only small-practice tool with trajectory-based failure alerts.

That is *good* news, correctly framed:

- It proves the feature is clinically valuable — decades of published evidence
- It costs ~₹21,000/year for the alerting alone, with no billing, no notes, and a research-grade interface
- It requires licensing individual instruments rather than using what you already have

**Aman's honest claim is not "nobody does this."** A sophisticated therapist knows about OQ-Analyst. The claim is: *the mechanism OQ-Analyst pioneered, without the 1990s interface, without running a second system, with your billing in the same place, at a third of the price.*

### What nobody has

Cohort-derived prediction that **improves as the platform grows**. OQ-Analyst's curves are fixed and derived from research samples. Aman's benchmark pool would be live and compounding. That is the genuinely unclaimed position — and it only works if the pool ships.

## 4a. Zoho One — the substitute threat

*Verified 31 July 2026 on zoho.com/one/pricing.*

| | Price |
|---|---|
| All-employee pricing, annual | **₹1,250/employee/month** (₹15,000/yr) |
| All-employee, monthly | ₹2,500/user |
| Flexible-user, annual | ₹3,500/user/month |
| Flexible-user, monthly | ₹4,000/user |

A solo practitioner is a one-person business and therefore qualifies for all-employee pricing: **₹1,250/month on annual commitment for 50+ integrated apps** — CRM, Books/Invoice, Bookings, Forms, Sign, Analytics, Campaigns, Mail, WorkDrive.

### Why this matters more than the vertical players

Zoho is Indian, DPDP-native, trusted by Indian small businesses, and already the default answer to "how do I run my business." A therapist can assemble a working practice stack — client records, scheduling, invoicing, intake forms, e-signature — for less than PractiPal charges for a single vertical tool.

**This is not a product competitor. It is a substitute** — the "I'll just build it myself" option. Its friction is real: Zoho requires configuration, and most therapists will not build CRM modules and wire up invoicing templates. That friction is precisely why PractiPal and PracFlow can exist at similar prices.

But it sets the **mental price anchor** for the Indian buyer. A therapist who knows Zoho One costs ₹1,250 for everything will find ₹1,999 for one vertical tool a harder sell than the global comparisons suggest.

### What Zoho cannot do

Nothing in the suite computes reliable change, clinically significant change, baseline deterioration, effect size, or a cohort-matched recovery trajectory. Zoho Forms can collect a score. It cannot tell you the score means the client is sliding.

**Net effect on strategy: Zoho strengthens the Pro-only argument.** It is the third independent confirmation — alongside PractiPal and PracFlow — that the billing-and-notes layer has no pricing power in India. Only the outcomes engine does.

## 5. Instrument licensing — verified position

| Instrument | Status |
|---|---|
| **PHQ-9, GAD-7** | Free to reproduce and administer without permission. The safe defaults |
| **PCL-5** | Public domain via the US National Center for PTSD |
| **OQ-45 family** | Requires OQ Measures licence, from $250/yr solo |
| **ORS / SRS** | Free for individual clinicians who register. **Organisational use licensed. Digital administration not covered by the free individual licence** |

This confirms the §2 position in the business plan. Build the portal around PHQ-9 and GAD-7 and both the licensing problem and the product problem resolve together.

## 6. The product gap — and it's serious

Every credible MBC platform delivers the measure **to the client, automatically, on a schedule**. Aman does not. The counsellor administers on paper and types the numbers in.

The category's own consensus is blunt about why this matters: outcome tracking only happens when the scale reaches the client automatically, and client completion is the failure mode of every measurement programme. The reason most therapists don't measure is friction — hand-scoring a PHQ-9 does not survive a busy caseload.

**Aman currently has that friction.** It works for Vijay because Vijay is committed. It will not survive contact with a Segment B therapist who was never going to score anything by hand.

### Consequence for the roadmap

The Phase 4 client portal was scoped as a token-gated payment-history view. **Re-scope it.** Its highest-value job is automated measure delivery: client gets a link before the session, completes PHQ-9 or GAD-7 in ninety seconds, the score lands in the note automatically.

This single change:
- Removes the adoption barrier for the entire Segment B market
- Makes the deterioration flags reliable rather than dependent on discipline
- Feeds the benchmark pool at a far higher rate
- Sidesteps the ORS/SRS problem entirely, because PHQ-9 and GAD-7 carry no digital-use restriction

**This should outrank AI note drafting and probably outranks the scheduling view.** It is the difference between a product that measures and a product that could measure.

## 7. Pricing recommendation

### The evidence

| Band | Price/month | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Indian generalist | ₹700–1,500 | Scheduling, billing, no measurement |
| Global generalist | ₹2,500–5,800 | Scheduling, billing, basic score tracking |
| **Global MBC specialist** | **₹2,100–3,400** | **Measurement only — no billing, no notes** |
| OQ-Analyst | ~₹1,750 (₹21,000/yr) | Trajectory alerting only |

**An Indian therapist who wants what Aman Pro does must currently buy Greenspace (₹2,100) plus PractiPal (₹1,499) — roughly ₹3,600/month — and still won't get trajectory prediction.**

### Recommended

| Tier | Monthly | Annual | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Practice** | **₹999** | **₹9,990** | Undercuts PractiPal (₹1,499) and PracFlow (₹1,199); above the Therasoft floor. Cheap without signalling unserious |
| **Pro** | **₹1,999** | **₹19,990** | Above every Indian tool — signals a different category. Below every global MBC tool. Roughly half the Greenspace + PractiPal stack |

### Why ₹499 was wrong

At ₹499 the Practice tier reads as unserious to exactly the sophisticated buyer you want converting to Pro, and there is no competitive need for it — ₹999 already undercuts every Indian competitor. You were giving away ₹500/month for nothing.

### Why ₹1,999 rather than ₹1,499 for Pro

₹1,499 places Pro at parity with a scheduling tool, which frames Aman as a PractiPal alternative rather than a different category. ₹1,999 breaks that frame while remaining a visible bargain against any global comparison.

### The anchor to use on the pricing page

> Greenspace, for measurement alone: ₹2,100/month.
> Add a practice-management tool for the billing: another ₹1,499.
> Aman Pro does both, plus trajectory prediction neither offers: **₹1,999.**

That makes ₹1,999 read as a saving rather than a premium. Verify both numbers on the day it ships.

## 8. Risks this research surfaces

| Risk | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Blueprint's free EHR** | 70,000+ professionals, free full EHR, strong MBC heritage. If they localise to India, the Practice tier is dead overnight. Pro survives on trajectory prediction. **Argument for making Pro the business, not Practice** |
| **Category absence in India may mean absent demand** | No Indian competitor does MBC. That is either a gap or a verdict. The 3-user beta is the cheapest way to find out — specifically whether a Segment B therapist will measure at all |
| **Carepatron's free tier** | Global, 120+ countries, free forever, includes India. A price-sensitive Indian therapist has a genuinely free option today |
| **OQ-Analyst comparison** | A sophisticated prospect may know it. Don't claim novelty — claim better packaging and better price. Overclaiming to this audience is fatal |

## 9. What changes in the plan

1. **Pricing** → ₹999 / ₹1,999 (from ₹499 / ₹1,499)
2. **Client portal re-scoped** to automated measure delivery and promoted above AI notes on the roadmap
3. **Comparison table on the home page** must include the global MBC specialists, not just Indian generalists — that is the honest comparison and the one that justifies the price
4. **Positioning language** shifts from "nobody does this" to "the mechanism the research gold standard pioneered, packaged for a working practice"
5. **Beta question sharpens** to: will a therapist who doesn't currently measure actually measure — and does automated delivery change that answer
