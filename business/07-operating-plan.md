# Aman — Operating Plan

*July 2026. Consolidates and replaces `aman-business-plan-v2.md`. Execution detail; strategy lives in `03-business-strategy.md`.*

---

## 1. The operating constraint

This is a **side product**. Revenue target is modest; the scarce resource is founder hours.

**Budget: 10–12 founder-hours per month at steady state.** (Revised up from 6 to accommodate the monthly webinar.)

Every decision is judged against: *does this add support load or sales load?* If yes, it needs an unusually good reason.

---

## 2. Discipline rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Self-serve signup only. **No demos, ever.** | Sales time is the largest hidden cost |
| A 6-minute Loom replaces every demo request | One artefact, unlimited uses |
| **Webinar is group-only. Never 1:1.** | The single most likely thing to consume the founder |
| Webinar capped at 60 minutes, once monthly | Hard ceiling on the only uncapped commitment |
| Email support only, published 48h SLA. **No WhatsApp.** | WhatsApp creates instant-response expectations |
| Every repeated question becomes a help-doc page, not a reply | Support load must be logarithmic, not linear |
| Published "we will never build this" list | Deflects roadmap requests without negotiation |
| One release window per month. No hotfix culture. | Protects evenings |
| Permanent one-click full export | Ethical obligation and churn de-risking |
| **Never take an organisational sale**, however large | See strategy §3.5 |

## 3. Go-to-market

### 3.1 Channels, in priority order

**1. Vijay's network — months 0–6, primary**
The only real distribution asset. One ask, ten warm introductions into his supervision group and peer circle. Nothing else runs until this is exhausted.

**2. Outcome-measurement and supervision communities — months 0–12**
Practitioner groups, supervision cohorts, psychology-department alumni networks (TISS, Christ, Amity, Fergusson). Small, dense, self-identifying — the best fit for the ICP. Participate; don't pitch.

**3. The free webinar — month 4 onward**
Recorded once, run monthly, open to anyone. Teaches routine outcome monitoring as a practice, instrument-agnostic. It is marketing, not a product: no fulfilment obligation, no support burden, infinitely repeatable. Doubles as the top of the Practice→Pro funnel.

**4. SEO content — months 4–24**
Slow-compounding, zero ongoing sales time. One post a month. Target the clinical question, not the admin one:
- "how do I know if my client is improving"
- "what to do when a client's scores drop"
- "deterioration in therapy — the minority nobody discusses"
- "reliable change explained for practitioners"
- "how do Indian counsellors decide what to charge"

Each ends with a free downloadable tool gated by email.

### 3.2 Ruled out

Paid ads · cold outreach · sales calls and demos · organisational and platform sales (permanently) · **Adi's existing audience** — 5,000 newsletter subscribers, IG and TEDx reach founders, students and educators, with near-zero overlap with practising counsellors. Do not assume transfer.

## 4. Beta — three users

### Shape
**3 solo counsellors · 60 days · free · no card · recruited only through Vijay's introductions.**

Vijay does not count as one of the three. He has shaped the product and cannot test whether a stranger can use it.

**At least two should not currently measure outcomes.** Recruiting three already-converted practitioners would feel like success and teach nothing about the Practice tier or the conversion path.

**Don't wait for the home page.** These arrive by warm introduction, not through the funnel. Recruit in parallel with the rebuild.

### Primary metric

> **Can someone who isn't Vijay reach their first invoice and their first outcome score without messaging the founder?**

With three users, statistical validation is off the table. This is the only thing three people can prove — and it's the thing that determines whether the business fits in ten hours a month.

### Interview questions, in priority order

1. **"In the last twelve months, what have you spent money on to become a better counsellor — and roughly how much?"**
   *The single most important question. The entire ₹1,999 thesis rests on the answer.*
2. **"What do you pay for supervision?"**
   *The primary pricing anchor and currently unverified.*
3. "Tell me about a client you didn't help."
   *The authenticity screen. The answer is the whole assessment.*
4. "Would you have set up Zoho yourself?"
   *Tests whether ease-of-setup is genuinely the Practice moat.*
5. "What did you think when you first saw a flag?"
   *Tests the medicine/poison line directly.*

### What to watch

- Whether either non-measuring user records a score voluntarily by week six
- Whether anyone opens the charts twice
- Time from signup to first invoice
- Which instrument each chooses when offered the choice
- Whether a flag ever changed a clinical decision — **the single most valuable testimonial available**
- Every support question, because with three users the support log *is* the research

### Conversion
At day 60: founding-member annual, ₹11,990 on Pro, locked three years. Target 2 of 3.

## 5. Milestones

| Window | Objective |
|---|---|
| **Months 0–3** | Strip instrument names from public copy · IP review · rename · onboarding under 5 minutes · privacy and data-ownership copy · 3 beta users onboarded |
| **Month 4** | Paid launch on both tiers · beta conversions · **automated measure delivery shipped** |
| **Months 4–6** | 25 paying · **Outcome Report shipped** · first webinar run · Vijay referral loop exhausted |
| **Months 6–9** | 50 paying · benchmark pool live with opt-in consent · webinar monthly |
| **Months 9–12** | Peer comparison in the Outcome Report · 10–12 founder-hours/month sustained |
| **Months 12–18** | **100 paying at ≥40% Pro mix (~₹1.1L/month)** |
| **Month 18+** | Hold steady, or reconsider scope |

### Build order — the only thing that matters

1. **Strip instrument names from public copy** — one hour, removes the only public legal exposure, blocks nothing
2. **Onboarding under five minutes** — Practice's entire moat
3. **Automated measure delivery** (re-scope the Phase 4 client portal) — without it, thing one doesn't work
4. **The Outcome Report** — without it, thing two doesn't exist
5. **Benchmark pool** — the only compounding moat

Everything else waits. AI note drafting is deprioritised indefinitely: commodity within a year, and not the differentiator.

## 6. North-star metric

> **Counsellors who have recorded outcome scores for four consecutive weeks.**

Not signups. Not MRR. Not conversion rate.

A counsellor who measures for a month keeps measuring, upgrades, generates reports, feeds the pool and refers peers. One who doesn't will churn regardless of what they're paying.

**Report weekly. Judge every product decision against it.**

Secondary: Practice→Pro conversion · benchmark pool opt-in rate · founder hours per month · support tickets per user.

## 7. Risk register

| # | Risk | Severity | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Aman reproduces the platform trauma it should cure** — outcome data read as verdict, not information | **High** | All five measures in strategy §3.5. Non-negotiable |
| 2 | **Outcome instrument digital licensing** | High | Pluggable instruments; unrestricted defaults; IP review before launch |
| 3 | **Clinical data and DPDP** | High | Encryption at rest; India residency; documented breach process; benchmark pool opt-in and de-identified. Note competitors publish four named certifications — understate rather than overclaim |
| 4 | **Automated delivery missing** — every measurement competitor has it | High | Ships in month 4. Until then, honest "on the roadmap" in the comparison table |
| 5 | **Pricing thesis rests on unvalidated willingness-to-pay** | High | Beta questions 1 and 2 before any public price |
| 6 | **Case-mix punishes hard caseloads** | High | Baseline-severity adjustment always. Never publish comparative rankings |
| 7 | Self-reported data is gameable | Medium–High | Report completeness alongside outcomes: "based on X of Y closed clients" |
| 8 | Blueprint or a funded player localises to India | Medium | Practice tier wouldn't survive it; Pro would. Argument for treating Pro as the business |
| 9 | Zoho's free stack caps Practice pricing permanently | Medium | Accepted. Practice competes on setup effort, not features |
| 10 | Single maintainer holding clinical records | Medium | Permanent one-click export, published as a trust feature |
| 11 | Clinical liability from a missed flag | Medium | Decision support, never diagnosis. Explicit disclaimer. Never auto-notify clients |
| 12 | Category absence in India may mean absent demand | Medium | The 3-user beta is the cheapest possible test |

## 8. Immediate actions

1. **Rename the product.** `practiceSettings` currently defaults `practiceName` to "Aman Counseling" and `counselorName` to Vijay's real name and city. A multi-tenant product cannot be named after one customer's clinic, and new tenants must not boot with a real person's details. Scrub the defaults in the same pass.
2. **Strip instrument names from all public copy.**
3. **IP review** of the pluggable-instrument model, before launch.
4. **Ask Vijay** what he pays for supervision, and what he spent last year on professional development.
5. **Ask Vijay for three introductions.**
6. Build the demo tenant with fabricated data; capture the three screenshots.
7. Privacy policy and data-ownership copy, in plain English.
8. Record the 6-minute Loom.
