Most counsellors work blind. On the money they're owed. And on the clients quietly getting worse.
deepen fixes both. Invoicing that runs itself, and a warning before a client slips away. Sold self-serve, grown by referral, run in ten founder-hours a month.
Pain 1 · why they sign up
"Month-end is spreadsheets, invoices made by hand, and awkward messages chasing payment. Some clients just disappear."
Your month closes in ten minutes.

Log sessions as you go. One click bills everyone. Rupee and dollar clients stay separate. Short sessions pro-rate themselves. Receipts, part-payments and credits are handled properly.

Admin is the loudest complaint in therapist burnout studies. It is not the deepest one.
Pain 2 · why they stay. Nobody in India does this
"Some clients get worse. You don't see it until they stop replying, and then you guess for a very long time."
You'll know by session three.

A quick score each session, measured against where that client started. When someone slides, the note carries a flag and says why. It is information about the client, never a verdict on the counsellor.

Predicted progress: this client against similar starters
Private by design. No employer, no platform, no supervisor ever sees it.

The affordability wall is the finding that matters. Most Indian therapists earn ₹3 lakh a year or less. Pro at ₹1,999 a month is ₹24,000 a year, which is 8% of their gross revenue. That is not a pricing objection to negotiate past. It is a structural ceiling that only moves when practitioner earnings move.
So India proves the product. It cannot make it big. Full penetration of India's payable solo market is ₹24 crore today and ₹54 crore by 2036. For scale, the combined FY24 revenue of India's ten best-known digital mental health platforms was about ₹103 crore. 100 paying users is 0.5% of the reachable base and entirely achievable. 10,000 would be a fifth of every solo counsellor in India we could reach, in 2036, on the base case. The path below is built on that.

Everything above, plus progress tracking, flags and predicted progress. Priced against supervision and training, which counsellors already pay for.
Today a counsellor buys measurement (₹2,100) and billing (₹1,499) separately. That is ₹3,600 a month, and still no prediction. Pro does both for ₹1,999.

The path to 100,000 users

Four stages. The first two are India. The last two cannot be, and the workforce numbers are why. Grown through the founders' clinical network, a free monthly webinar, and content that compounds. No cold outreach, no demos, no selling to organisations.
India · months 0–18
100
Prove the habit. 0.5% of the reachable base. Three-user beta, paid launch at month 4, break-even at 59. ₹1.1L a month. Still a side product.
India · years 2–3
1,000
Own the beachhead. About 5% of everyone in India we can reach and who can pay. Roughly ₹1.3Cr a year. This is close to India's practical ceiling, and it is the fork.
English-speaking · years 3–5
10,000
Go abroad, because India realistically cannot supply this. Dollar pricing is already built, and the affordability wall does not exist in those markets. Needs HIPAA and GDPR, a team of five.
Global · years 5–8
100,000
Own the category. The benchmark pool improves with every user and incumbents run fixed curves. About $12M a year at a blended $10.
The honest gate. Stages three and four abandon the side-product life, and they are not optional if the goal is size. India is where this gets proven and where the product earns its clinical credibility. It is not where the revenue is. That decision gets taken at the 1,000 mark with real numbers in hand, not before. North star throughout: counsellors who have recorded scores four weeks running.
Honesty line. No revenue today. One practice live, a co-founder's. Every projection is a target at a 60/40 Practice/Pro mix, not a forecast. Pricing stays unproven until the beta answers one question: what did you spend last year to become a better counsellor? Stages two to four depend on stage one working. Name decided: Deepen.
