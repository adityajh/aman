Most counsellors work blind. On the money they're owed. And on the clients quietly getting worse.
Deepen, outcome tracking for counsellors. It fixes both. Invoicing that runs itself, and a warning before a client slips away. Sold self-serve, grown by referral, run in ten founder-hours a month.
Pain 1 · why they sign up
"Month-end is spreadsheets, invoices made by hand, and awkward messages chasing payment. Some clients just disappear."
Your month closes in ten minutes.

Log sessions as you go. One click bills everyone. Rupee and dollar clients stay separate. Short sessions pro-rate themselves. Receipts, part-payments and credits are handled properly.

Admin is the loudest complaint in therapist burnout studies. It is not the deepest one.

"Some clients get worse. You don't see it until they stop replying, and then you guess for a very long time."
You'll know by session three.

A quick score each session, measured against where that client started. When someone slides, the note carries a flag and says why. It is information about the client, never a verdict on the counsellor.

The 98% is the customer. India graduates 14,759 psychology postgraduates a year against roughly 290 clinical psychology seats. Two per cent can ever be licensed; the rest go into unregulated counselling with no supervisor, no institutional quality signal and no way to know whether they are any good. The regulatory gap that makes this market impossible to count is the same gap that creates the need.
The affordability wall is the finding that matters. Most Indian therapists earn ₹3 lakh a year or less. Pro at ₹1,999 a month is ₹24,000 a year — 8% of gross revenue. That is not a pricing objection to negotiate past. It is a structural ceiling that only moves when practitioner earnings move.

What moves the ceiling

The scenarios differ on whether a payer appears, not on training capacity. India already produces far more psychology postgraduates than practice absorbs.

2036 figures at full penetration of the payable solo market. Every historical episode of fast workforce growth — UK IAPT, Australia's Better Access, the US post-parity decade — was triggered by a payer, not a training expansion. The thing that grows this market is the same event that defines the third column.
So India proves the product. It cannot make it big. Full penetration of the payable solo market is ₹24 crore today and ₹54 crore by 2036 on the base case. For scale, the combined FY24 revenue of India's ten best-known digital mental health platforms was about ₹103 crore. 100 paying users is 0.5% of the reachable base and entirely achievable. 10,000 would be a fifth of every solo counsellor in India we could reach, in 2036. The path below is built on that.
Why the ceiling is not in India. Roughly 600,000 therapists in the US and 75,000 in the UK, in markets where the affordability wall does not exist. India is where the product earns its clinical credibility and its data. It is not where the revenue is.

The path to 100,000 users

Four stages. The first two are India. The last two cannot be, and the workforce numbers are why. Grown through the founders' clinical network, a free monthly webinar, and content that compounds. No cold outreach, no demos, no selling to organisations.
India · months 0–18
100
Prove the habit. 0.5% of the reachable base. Three-user beta, paid launch at month 4, break-even at 59. ₹1.1L a month. Still a side product.

1,000
Own the beachhead. About 5% of everyone in India we can reach and who can pay. Roughly ₹1.3Cr a year. This is close to India's practical ceiling, and it is the fork.
English-speaking · years 3–5
10,000
Go abroad, because India realistically cannot supply this. Dollar pricing is already built, and the affordability wall does not exist in those markets. Needs HIPAA and GDPR, a team of five.
Global · years 5–8
100,000
Own the category. The comparison pool improves with every user while incumbents run fixed curves. About $12M a year at a blended $10, plus a second line: licensing our own outcome measure to other vendors.
The honest gate. Stages three and four abandon the side-product life, and they are not optional if the goal is size. That decision gets taken at the 1,000 mark with real numbers in hand, not before. The one thing that could change it is a payer event in India — enforced parity, a subsidised session scheme, or Tele-MANAS paying panel counsellors. That moves the ceiling from ₹54 crore to ₹132 crore and is entirely outside our control, so we plan without it and watch for it. North star throughout: counsellors who have recorded scores four weeks running.
Honesty line. No revenue today. One practice live, a co-founder's. Every projection is a target at a 60/40 Practice/Pro mix, not a forecast. Pricing stays unproven until the beta answers one question: what did you spend last year to become a better counsellor? Stages two to four depend on stage one working. Name decided: Deepen, domains registered, trademark search pending. The outcome measure is being brought in-house — the scales in the product today are third-party and are being replaced with our own, which is also what makes the licensing line in stage four possible.
