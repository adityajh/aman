Can the depth visual be the mark?

The idea is right. The current drawing is not a logo, it is an illustration. Here is the test it fails, three reductions that pass, and a recommendation.

Why the current visual cannot be the logo

It dies at 16px. Seven bands inside a favicon is mud. The brand guide's own rule: works at 16px, works in one colour, works stamped in ink on the corner of a PDF.

It cannot go one-colour. The whole meaning is carried by the gradient. Strip the colour for a fax, an embroidery, a black-and-white invoice, and nothing is left.

It is a diagram, not a mark. The lead weight and line explain an idea. A logo does not explain, it identifies.

Keep it anyway, as the brand's illustration. Use it on the site, in the deck, in these documents. Brand illustration and logo mark are different jobs and most good systems have both.

The control

No mark at all. This is a serious option, not a placeholder.
deepen.
Serif wordmark, teal ink, terracotta full stop. One ornament, no mark.

A two-person product does not need a symbol. Wordmark-only is disciplined, costs nothing to maintain, and never looks amateur. The only place it genuinely hurts is the favicon and the app icon, where a "d" would have to stand in.

Recommendation

What I would do

Wordmark as the primary identity. Mark C as the icon. The wordmark leads everywhere it fits: site header, invoice footer, the Outcome Report, email. Mark C exists for the places a wordmark cannot go, which is the favicon, the app icon, and the corner of a PDF.

Keep the surface-to-depth illustration as illustration. It is doing good work in these documents and it belongs on the site. It just is not the mark.

Retire the bezel. The old brand guide proposed a compass bezel with an index tick. That was Fathom's navigation metaphor and the rename orphaned it. Mark C replaces it cleanly.

One risk worth naming: a circle half-filled is not a rare shape. Check it against a battery icon, a coffee cup and a moon phase before committing, and consider raising the waterline slightly so it never sits at exactly half.
