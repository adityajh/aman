The one thing to hold onto. This product hands a counsellor numbers about her own effectiveness. Numbers like that can help a person or shame them, and the difference is entirely in how they are framed and who controls them. The brand exists to keep it on the helpful side. Every rule in this document traces back to that.

02 · Audience

Who we are speaking to

One counsellor, working alone, at the end of a long day.

She runs a small private practice. She is good at the work and has almost no way of proving it, to herself or anyone else. She has no supervisor she can afford to see often, no peer group that talks in numbers, and no idea whether her results are normal.

The thing that shapes this entire brand: many counsellors have already met outcome measurement in its worst form. Scores demanded by an employer or a platform, reported upward, and read back as a verdict on their competence. For someone with that history, a product full of charts about her performance is not a neutral offer. It is a reminder.

So we are not selling measurement. We are offering a counsellor a private mirror that nobody else can look into, and we say so early and plainly.

What she must never feel

Every surface should read as
  • Information she holds and controls
  • Curiosity, not examination
  • About a client, never about her worth
  • Measured against that client's own starting point
  • Private by default and by architecture
Never
  • A score, a grade, a rank or a league table
  • Visible to an employer, a platform or a supervisor
  • Compared against other counsellors
  • Attached to any consequence
  • Framed as performance
03 · The standing test

One question governs everything

Design reviews, copy reviews, feature decisions. This is the gate.

Would a counsellor who was once shamed by outcome metrics feel safe opening this?
If a screen, a phrase, a colour or an icon fails this, it does not ship. Regardless of how well it converts.

Four things we will not build, at any price

These are not a roadmap discussion. They are settled, and the reason is the same each time: each one turns a private mirror into a public verdict.

Any view for an employer, platform or supervisorThe moment someone above her can see it, the meaning inverts
Rankings, league tables or comparison between counsellorsTurns hard cases into a liability and rewards cream-skimming
Public badges, seals or a practitioner directoryMakes outcome data a marketing asset, which corrupts the data
Streaks, scores, grades or gamification of any kindSomeone who internalises client failure will internalise a broken streak
04 · Name

deepen.

Not a word we invented. A word the audience already uses for the thing they are trying to do.

Deepen the process

The clinical sense. Helping a client move past surface content to what is actually underneath. A named skill, taught and studied.

Deepen your practice

The professional development phrase. What certifications, workshops and supervision all promise. A category counsellors already invest in.

Deepen the picture

What the product does. Every session adds a reading, and the view of a client, and of a career, gets less shallow over time.

Why it works. The name describes the work, not the software. No verdict is possible inside it: to deepen assumes you are already good and offers you further. And the counsellor is always the subject. She deepens. The product never deepens her.

The three lines, and when each is used

They do different jobs. Keep them apart and never blend them into one sentence.

Go deeper than you could alone.
Tagline		 Public and emotional. Sits under the wordmark, opens the site, closes a talk. Use where a person is meeting the brand for the first time.
Deepen, outcome tracking for counsellors.
Descriptor		 Functional and searchable. Title tags, app listings, social bios, the line after the name in an introduction. Never ship the name alone in metadata.
Empowering mental health practitioners.
Mission		 Internal north star. Decides what gets built. It can appear publicly, but it is not a sales line and should never do a tagline's job.

Why the tagline earns its place. The deepest thing counsellors in solo practice report is not admin and it is not money. It is working without a reference point. No supervisor they can afford to see often, no peer group that talks in numbers, no way of knowing whether their experience is normal. Alone is the word that names that, and the line answers it without ever saying the word lonely.

It is also literally true, which is what keeps it honest. A counsellor cannot see a recovery pattern from inside one practice. The comparison that makes prediction possible draws on more than she has. She really can go deeper than she could alone.

Two ways to get it wrong. Do not turn it into a command with an exclamation mark. And do not let "alone" drift into a jab at how she works now: the line offers company, it does not criticise solitude.

How to write it

✓deepen.The wordmark. Lowercase, serif, terracotta full stop.
✓DeepenIn running text, sentence case, no full stop. "Deepen flagged her last week."
✓Deepen, outcome tracking for counsellorsThe descriptor. Use in titles, listings and bios.
✗DEEPENNever all caps, never letterspaced.
✗Deepen™, DeepenApp, Deepen.ioNo symbols, no suffixes, no domain in the name.
✗"a practice management tool"Never. That phrase invites a comparison with free generic software and loses it.
05 · Voice

Plain, specific, unhurried, adult

Write as if speaking to one experienced professional who is tired and does not want to be sold to.

1
Concede before you claim

Name what we do not do, and name who does it better. With clinicians, conceding is what makes the wins believable.

2
Say what it does, never what it proves

Shows, flags, tracks, compares, tells you. Never improves, optimises, transforms, unlocks.

3
One counsellor, second person

Not a market, not a segment. Short sentences. No exclamation marks, anywhere, ever.

In practice

Do not write

Unlock powerful insights into your clinical performance.

Our proprietary algorithm predicts client outcomes with industry-leading accuracy.

Never miss an at-risk client again!

Write

See whether your clients are actually getting better.

We compare this client against people who started in a similar place. When there is not enough history to say anything useful, we say so.

When someone slides, the note carries a flag and tells you why.

Two lines that must appear wherever they apply

Above any practice-level outcome figures: these numbers describe a caseload, not a clinician. Harder caseloads produce harder numbers.

Wherever prediction appears: when there is not enough history to say something useful, the product says so rather than guessing. Never soften this into a hedge. It is a feature.

06 · Logo

Wordmark leads. One mark for everywhere else.

Three horizontal bars, thickening as they descend. A hairline on top is the surface. Two solid blocks below are the depth.

At size. The 16px build thickens the bars so the hairline holds.
Construction. 64 unit grid. Bars at x=10, width 44, corner radius 2. Heights 4, 9 and 12 at y=13, 24 and 39. Vertical span centred on 32. Gaps tighten going down, 7 then 6, so compression reads as depth. At 16px, bars thicken to 5, 10 and 13.

Single tone, always. Every bar is the same colour. Thickness carries the whole idea and tone carries none of it. A tonal version puts the top hairline at roughly 2:1 against paper, too faint to survive at small sizes. Single tone means the mark is structurally identical everywhere and can never fail contrast.

Clear space. On all four sides, leave at least the height of the bottom bar. Nothing sits inside that margin.

Minimum size. Mark 16px. Lockup 90px wide. Below that, use the mark alone.

Which to use where. The wordmark leads everywhere it fits: site header, invoice footer, reports, email. The mark appears only where a wordmark cannot go, which is the favicon, the app icon and the corner of a document. Never set both on the same surface.

Misuse

Never
Equal bars. That is a menu icon.
Never
Varying widths. That is a bar chart.
Never
Gradients, shadows or effects.
Never
Rotate, stretch or recolour freely.

Also never: outline the bars, place the mark on a photograph or a busy field, put it inside a container shape it was not designed for, or animate it.

Most of any surface is paper and ink. Teal is roughly one part in twelve and terracotta is a trace. If a screen looks colourful, it is wrong. The only thing that should draw the eye is the data.

The depth ramp

surfacedepth

One hue, light to dark, for any continuous magnitude. Light is what a single reading tells you. Dark is what the accumulated pattern tells you.

The terracotta rule

Terracotta belongs to welcomes, onboarding, empty states, the wordmark's full stop, and one chart series. Never on a verdict, never on a flag, never on a button. If terracotta ever tells a counsellor something about her numbers, it is being misused.

Accessibility

Every foreground colour clears 4.5:1 against its intended surface, which is WCAG AA for body text. Nothing meaningful is carried by hue alone.

09 · Data and charts

The chart is the brand

The most important thing this product shows anyone is a chart about a real person. It gets the most discipline.

Depth is the brand. Direction is the data. Never mix them.

A counsellor going deeper is the promise. A client's scores going down is deterioration. Both are "down" and they mean opposite things. The resolution is that depth here means insight, not submersion. Going deeper with a client, depth of understanding, depth psychology: all native to this profession and all good. The distress idioms belong to water, not depth. So the brand uses depth and never uses water.

The consequence. Charts keep clinical convention: a higher score is better wellbeing, and the line goes up when someone improves. The depth metaphor lives in the interface, never in the data. If a chart ever implies deeper is better for a client, it is wrong.

Verdicts

Ahead Tracking Worth a closer look
The product never celebrates loudly and never shames loudly. It points.

Only "worth a closer look" earns a colour, and it is brick rather than alarm red. Saturated red tells someone primed to internalise failure that she did something wrong. Brick says look at this. Status never carries meaning by colour alone: always an icon and a label with it.

Every screenshot, in every context, uses fabricated demonstration data. Never a real client, never a real name, not even blurred.

10 · Imagery

Show the work, not the feeling

The default hero image is a chart on demonstration data. It is the most honest picture of what the product does.

Use
  • Product surfaces on fabricated data
  • The depth ramp as a flat illustration device
  • Plain diagrams in brand colours, one idea each
  • Generous white space around everything
  • If people appear at all: real practitioners, unposed, at work
Never
  • Stock photography of smiling professionals
  • Anyone appearing distressed or vulnerable
  • Sage and blush gradients, lotus, leaves, candles
  • Brains, heads with cogs, glowing neural networks
  • Purple gradients, glassmorphism, floating blobs, sparkle emoji

Illustration is not the logo. The layered depth drawing is a brand illustration. It belongs on the site and in documents. It is not a mark, it does not go in a favicon, and it never replaces the logo.

A note on why this guide is strict. Most brand rules exist to keep things tidy. Several of these exist to keep a person safe. A counsellor who reads a flag as an accusation, or sees her caseload described as her performance, does not file a complaint. She quietly stops using the product, and she is right to. That is the failure this document is built to prevent.
