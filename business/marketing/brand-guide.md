Outcome tracking for counsellors.
Mission: empowering mental health practitioners.

**The one thing to hold onto.** This product hands a counsellor numbers about her own effectiveness. Numbers like that can help a person or shame them, and the difference is entirely in how they are framed and who controls them. The brand exists to keep it on the helpful side. Every rule here traces back to that.

## 02 · Audience

One counsellor, working alone, at the end of a long day. She runs a small private practice, is good at the work, and has almost no way of proving it to herself or anyone else. No supervisor she can afford to see often, no peer group that talks in numbers, no idea whether her results are normal.

**What shapes this entire brand:** many counsellors have already met outcome measurement in its worst form. Scores demanded by an employer or a platform, reported upward, read back as a verdict on their competence. For someone with that history, a product full of charts about her performance is not a neutral offer. It is a reminder.

So we are not selling measurement. We offer a private mirror nobody else can look into, and we say so early and plainly.

| Every surface should read as | Never |
|---|---|
| Information she holds and controls | A score, grade, rank or league table |
| Curiosity, not examination | Visible to an employer, platform or supervisor |
| About a client, never about her worth | Compared against other counsellors |
| Measured against that client's own starting point | Attached to any consequence |
| Private by default and by architecture | Framed as performance |

---

## 03 · The standing test

> **Would a counsellor who was once shamed by outcome metrics feel safe opening this?**

If a screen, a phrase, a colour or an icon fails this, it does not ship. Regardless of how well it converts.

### Four things we will not build, at any price

| | Why |
|---|---|
| Any view for an employer, platform or supervisor | The moment someone above her can see it, the meaning inverts |
| Rankings, league tables, comparison between counsellors | Turns hard cases into a liability and rewards cream-skimming |
| Public badges, seals or a practitioner directory | Makes outcome data a marketing asset, which corrupts the data |
| Streaks, scores, grades or gamification | Someone who internalises client failure will internalise a broken streak |

## 04 · Name

**deepen.** Not a word we invented. A word the audience already uses for the thing they are trying to do.

| Sense | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Deepen the process** | The clinical sense. Helping a client move past surface content to what is underneath. A named skill, taught and studied |
| **Deepen your practice** | The professional development phrase. What certifications, workshops and supervision all promise |
| **Deepen the picture** | What the product does. Every session adds a reading, and the view gets less shallow over time |

The name describes the work, not the software. No verdict is possible inside it: to deepen assumes you are already good and offers you further. And the counsellor is always the subject. She deepens. The product never deepens her.

### The three lines, and when each is used

They do different jobs. Keep them apart and never blend them into one sentence.

| Line | Job |
|---|---|
| ***Go deeper than you could alone.*** · tagline | Public and emotional. Under the wordmark, opening the site, closing a talk. Where someone meets the brand for the first time |
| **Deepen, outcome tracking for counsellors.** · descriptor | Functional and searchable. Title tags, app listings, bios, the line after the name in an introduction. Never ship the name alone in metadata |
| **Empowering mental health practitioners.** · mission | Internal north star. Decides what gets built. May appear publicly, but never does a tagline's job |

**Why the tagline earns its place.** The deepest thing counsellors in solo practice report is not admin and it is not money. It is working without a reference point. *Alone* is the word that names it, and the line answers it without ever saying lonely.

It is also literally true, which is what keeps it honest. A counsellor cannot see a recovery pattern from inside one practice. The comparison that makes prediction possible draws on more than she has. She really can go deeper than she could alone.

**Two ways to get it wrong.** Do not turn it into a command with an exclamation mark. And do not let "alone" drift into a jab at how she works now: the line offers company, it does not criticise solitude.

### How to write it

| | |
|---|---|
| ✓ deepen. | The wordmark. Lowercase, serif, terracotta full stop |
| ✓ Deepen | In running text, sentence case, no full stop. "Deepen flagged her last week." |
| ✓ Deepen, outcome tracking for counsellors | The descriptor |
| ✗ DEEPEN | Never all caps, never letterspaced |
| ✗ Deepen™, DeepenApp, Deepen.io | No symbols, suffixes, or domains in the name |
| ✗ "a practice management tool" | Invites a comparison with free generic software and loses it |

## 05 · Voice

Plain, specific, unhurried, adult. Write as if speaking to one experienced professional who is tired and does not want to be sold to.

1. **Concede before you claim.** Name what we do not do, and who does it better. With clinicians, conceding is what makes the wins believable.
2. **Say what it does, never what it proves.** Shows, flags, tracks, compares, tells you. Never improves, optimises, transforms, unlocks.
3. **One counsellor, second person.** Not a market. Short sentences. No exclamation marks, anywhere, ever.

### Lexicon

| Say | Never |
|---|---|
| your outcome measure · the scale you already use | the name of any specific instrument in public copy |
| a flag · something to look at · worth a closer look | an alert · a warning · a red flag |
| behind where similar clients were | failing · underperforming · at risk |
| your practice's numbers · what your caseload looks like | your performance · your score · your rating |
| records are isolated at the database level | any architecture term the reader has to decode |
| counsellor | user · provider · clinician-partner |
| nobody else ever sees this | secure · enterprise-grade · bank-level encryption |

### In practice

| Do not write | Write |
|---|---|
| Unlock powerful insights into your clinical performance. | See whether your clients are actually getting better. |
| Our proprietary algorithm predicts client outcomes with industry-leading accuracy. | We compare this client against people who started in a similar place. When there is not enough history to say anything useful, we say so. |
| Never miss an at-risk client again! | When someone slides, the note carries a flag and tells you why. |

### Two lines that must appear wherever they apply

- **Above any practice-level outcome figures:** these numbers describe a caseload, not a clinician. Harder caseloads produce harder numbers.
- **Wherever prediction appears:** when there is not enough history to say something useful, the product says so rather than guessing. Never soften this into a hedge. It is a feature.

## 06 · Logo

**Three horizontal bars, thickening as they descend.** A hairline on top is the surface. Two solid blocks below are the depth.

### Construction

- 64 unit grid. Bars at `x=10`, width `44`, corner radius `2`.
- Heights **4, 9, 12** at `y=13, 24, 39`. Vertical span centred on 32.
- Gaps tighten going down, 7 then 6, so compression reads as depth.
- At 16px, bars thicken to **5, 10, 13** so the hairline holds.

### Rules

**Single tone, always.** Every bar is the same colour. Thickness carries the whole idea and tone carries none of it. A tonal version puts the top hairline at roughly 2:1 against paper, too faint to survive at small sizes. Single tone means the mark is structurally identical everywhere and can never fail contrast.

| Context | Colour | Contrast |
|---|---|---|
| Light surfaces | `#0B4F43` | 9.1:1 |
| Dark surfaces | `#5FC3AE` | 8.4:1 |
| One colour, print and stamps | `#14181C` | 17.1:1 |

- **Clear space.** On all four sides, at least the height of the bottom bar.
- **Minimum size.** Mark 16px. Lockup 90px wide. Below that, mark alone.
- **Which where.** The wordmark leads everywhere it fits: site header, invoice footer, reports, email. The mark appears only where a wordmark cannot go: favicon, app icon, corner of a document. Never both on one surface.

### Misuse

Never equal bars (that is a menu icon). Never varying widths (that is a bar chart). Never gradients, shadows or effects. Never rotate, stretch or recolour freely. Never outline the bars, place the mark on a photograph or busy field, put it in a container it was not designed for, or animate it.

## 07 · Colour

**Deep teal · fired clay · unbleached paper.** Teal sits between blue and green, which is trust meeting growth, or knowing meeting getting better. Terracotta is Indian warmth, for human moments only. Unbleached paper and ink keep the register of a field instrument rather than an app.

### Core roles

| Role | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| Surface, page | `#FBFAF7` | `#16181A` |
| Surface, card | `#FFFFFF` | `#1E2124` |
| Primary ink | `#14181C` | `#F5F4F1` |
| Secondary ink | `#4A5157` | `#B9BDC2` |
| Muted, captions and axes | `#878D93` | `#878D93` |
| Hairline | `#E4E1DA` | `#2B2F33` |
| Teal ink, headings and wordmark | `#0B4F43` | `#5FC3AE` |
| Teal action, buttons and data | `#0F8A72` | `#2AA890` |
| Terracotta, warmth only | `#C2632A` | `#CE7440` |

### Proportion

Roughly 70% paper and card, 20% ink, 8% teal, 2% terracotta. **If a screen looks colourful, it is wrong.** The only thing that should draw the eye is the data.

### The depth ramp

`#DDF0EB` → `#A8DACD` → `#63BCA9` → `#2AA890` → `#0F8A72` → `#0B5F50` → `#083F35`

One hue, light to dark, for any continuous magnitude. Light is what a single reading tells you. Dark is what the accumulated pattern tells you.

### The terracotta rule

Terracotta belongs to welcomes, onboarding, empty states, the wordmark's full stop, and one chart series. Never on a verdict, never on a flag, never on a button. **If terracotta ever tells a counsellor something about her numbers, it is being misused.**

### Accessibility

Every foreground colour clears 4.5:1 against its intended surface. Nothing meaningful is carried by hue alone.

## 08 · Typography

- **Newsreader**, weights 400 to 600, for headlines and editorial. Lowercase or sentence case. Never all caps, never letterspaced.
- **Inter** for interface, data and everything else. No serif inside a chart, ever.

## 09 · Data and charts

The most important thing this product shows anyone is a chart about a real person. It gets the most discipline.

### Depth is the brand. Direction is the data. Never mix them.

A counsellor going deeper is the promise. A client's scores going down is deterioration. Both are "down" and they mean opposite things.

The resolution: depth here means insight, not submersion. Going deeper with a client, depth of understanding, depth psychology, all native to this profession and all good. The distress idioms belong to water, not depth. **So the brand uses depth and never uses water.**

**The consequence.** Charts keep clinical convention: a higher score is better wellbeing, the line goes up when someone improves. The depth metaphor lives in the interface, never in the data. If a chart ever implies deeper is better for a client, it is wrong.

### Series order, fixed

**teal `#0F8A72` → violet `#8A5BA8` → terracotta `#C2632A` → blue `#1F5FA8`**
Dark: `#2AA890` → `#A97FC4` → `#CE7440` → `#4A85CE`

- Slot one is always the client's own line.
- First three are safe in any chart form.
- Slot four needs direct labels alongside colour.
- Past four, fold to Other or facet. Never a fifth hue.
- The cohort band is neutral fill, never a series.
- One y-axis, always. Never two scales.
- Thin marks, recessive grid, hover layer by default.

### Verdicts

| Verdict | Treatment | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Ahead | Quiet ink chip, small teal check | none |
| Tracking | Brass dot and label | `#B8860B` (dark `#D4A32B`) |
| Worth a closer look | Brick dot and label | `#B4472F` (dark `#D06A50`) |

**The product never celebrates loudly and never shames loudly. It points.** Only "worth a closer look" earns a colour, and it is brick rather than alarm red. Saturated red tells someone primed to internalise failure that she did something wrong. Status never carries meaning by colour alone: always an icon and a label with it.

Every screenshot, everywhere, uses fabricated demonstration data. Never a real client, never a real name, not even blurred.

## 10 · Imagery

The default hero image is a chart on demonstration data. It is the most honest picture of what the product does.

| Use | Never |
|---|---|
| Product surfaces on fabricated data | Stock photography of smiling professionals |
| The depth ramp as a flat illustration device | Anyone appearing distressed or vulnerable |
| Plain diagrams in brand colours, one idea each | Sage and blush gradients, lotus, leaves, candles |
| Generous white space | Brains, heads with cogs, glowing neural networks |
| Real practitioners, unposed, at work | Purple gradients, glassmorphism, blobs, sparkle emoji |

**Illustration is not the logo.** The layered depth drawing is a brand illustration. It belongs on the site and in documents. It is not a mark, it does not go in a favicon, and it never replaces the logo.

## 11 · Applications

| Surface | The one thing that matters |
|---|---|
| Home page | The chart above the fold. The headline positions, the chart proves |
| Product interface | Paper and ink. The only colour on screen should be the data |
| Invoice email | The counsellor's practice name leads. We are a small footer mark. It is her invoice to her client |
| Outcome report | The most designed artefact we make, because it goes to a supervisor. Methodology note, generated date, completeness line, caseload framing |
| Teaching content | Instrument-agnostic, useful on its own, no product pitch in the body |
| Error and empty states | Warm, brief, never apologetic twice. Terracotta is allowed here |

**Dark mode is a first-class surface.** Counsellors write notes after their last session of the day. Same system, stepped for a dark surface and checked against it.

## 12 · Governance

| Item | |
|---|---|
| Version | 1.0, August 2026 |
| Owner | The founders. Any change needs both |
| Assets | Five SVG files: lockup, mark in teal, ink and reversed, plus a small-size favicon build |
| Locked | The standing test, the four things we will not build, the terracotta rule, the depth-and-direction rule, the logo construction, the chart series order |
| Open to revision | Type scale, application rules, imagery examples, anything not locked |
| Colour changes | Any new or altered colour is checked for contrast against both surfaces before it ships. Do not eyeball it |
| Proposing a change | Say what breaks if we do not change it. If nothing breaks, the answer is no |

**A note on why this guide is strict.** Most brand rules exist to keep things tidy. Several of these exist to keep a person safe. A counsellor who reads a flag as an accusation, or sees her caseload described as her performance, does not file a complaint. She quietly stops using the product, and she is right to. That is the failure this document is built to prevent.
