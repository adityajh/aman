## §2 — THE PROBLEM

### Heading
**Nobody can tell you if you're any good at this.**

### Body
Your supervisor, if you have one and can afford one, sees a fraction of your work. Your clients tell you what they think you want to hear. Your booking calendar tells you how busy you are, which is not the same thing.

And some clients get worse in therapy. Not many — but some. Without measurement, that's usually invisible until they stop replying to messages, and you're left guessing what happened for a very long time.

### Close
You don't need a research department. You need a few numbers each session and something that reads them properly.

## §3 — DETERIORATION FLAGS

### Heading
**You'll know by session three.**

### Body
`[PRODUCT]` watches every score against that client's own starting point — not against an average, not against anyone else. When wellbeing drops past the threshold you set, or the working relationship weakens, the session note carries a flag and tells you exactly why in plain language.

It isn't a judgement about your work. It's a prompt to look at one client more closely, while there's still time to do something about it.

## §4 — PREDICTED PROGRESS *(hero feature — most space)*

### Heading
**Is this client on track, or just not there yet?**

### Body
The hardest question in practice, and the one you usually answer on instinct.

`[PRODUCT]` compares this client against everyone who started in a similar place: a shaded band of what recovery has typically looked like from there, their own line drawn over it, and a straight verdict — ahead, tracking, or behind.

Slow progress from a difficult starting point is not the same as stalled progress. This tells you which one you're looking at.

### The honesty beat *(keep this — it converts)*
When there isn't enough history to say anything useful, `[PRODUCT]` says so. It won't guess.

### The precedent paragraph *(keep this — it disarms the informed reader)*
This isn't a new idea. Trajectory-based feedback has thirty years of research behind it, and one serious tool built around it — priced for institutions, built like a research instrument, and running alongside whatever else you use. `[PRODUCT]` brings the same mechanism into the system where you already log your sessions and send your invoices.

### Visual
Full-width chart. Three verdict states as small variants.

## §10 — FAQ

**I don't measure outcomes at the moment. Is this for me?**
Start on Practice — you get your notes and billing sorted either way. Pro is there when you're curious. There's a free session on getting started with outcome measurement if you'd like to try before you decide.

**Which measure do I use?**
Whichever you're licensed to use, or one of the built-in options. `[PRODUCT]` reads any brief sessional scale — you configure it once and forget about it.

**Does this replace my calendar?**
No, deliberately. Keep using whatever you use.

**Do you have scheduling, reminders, or a client booking page?**
No. If those are your main problem, PractiPal or PracFlow will serve you better than we will, and we'd rather say so now.

**Can my employer or supervisor see my data?**
No. There is no mechanism for it and we don't sell to organisations. See above.

**What happens if I stop paying?**
You export everything in one click. Nothing is held hostage.

**Do my clients see any of this?**
Only if you choose to show them.

**How is this different from the analytics in my current tool?**
Theirs measure your business — revenue, retention, cancellations. This measures your clients.
